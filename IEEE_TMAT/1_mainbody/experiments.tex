Two metal--ferroelectric--metal stacks were compared: Stack~A, TaN/\hzo{}(10~nm)/TiN, and Stack~B, TiN/PE-TiN/\hzo{}/WO$_x$/W, where the latter introduces a deliberate bottom interfacial layer~\cite{paul2026wox}. Grazing-incidence XRD was used to confirm crystallization, and frequency-resolved $C$--$V$ measurements were acquired in parallel $C_p$--$R_p$ mode with a 50~mV rms probe from 1~kHz to 4~MHz. The dc bias was swept $-2 \rightarrow +2 \rightarrow -2$~V so that the two polarization branches were recorded under identical conditions.

For each frequency, the zero-bias capacitive memory window was extracted from the difference between the branch permittivities,
\begin{equation}
\CMW_\xi(f)=\xi_\downarrow(0,f)-\xi_\uparrow(0,f),
\label{eq:cmw_short}
\end{equation}
where $\xi_\uparrow$ and $\xi_\downarrow$ are the branches reached after opposite poling. PUND and switching-current measurements were then used to verify that the polarization state remained switchable independently of the capacitive readout. The temperature dependence was tested by repeating the electrical measurements at 300~K and 10~K with the same probe and instrument configuration. Main-text interpretation was restricted to frequencies below the series-resistance-limited regime identified from the high-frequency roll-off~\cite{nicollian1982,schroder2006}.
