\title{Interfacial Maxwell--Wagner Relaxation Limits Zero-Bias Capacitive Readout in Ferroelectric \hzo{} Capacitors}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Apu Das and Sourav De}
\IEEEauthorblockA{College of Semiconductor Research, National Tsing Hua University, Hsinchu 300044, Taiwan\\
Email: apudas@nthu.edu.tw, sourav.de@mx.nthu.edu.tw}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The capacitive memory window (CMW) of ferroelectric capacitors enables non-destructive readout, yet the mechanism that sets its usable bandwidth and temperature range remains unclear. We condense the full TMAT manuscript into a conference format by keeping the complete plot set and the core experimental comparison between TaN/\hzo{}/TiN and TiN/PE-TiN/\hzo{}/WO$_x$/W capacitors. Frequency-resolved $C$--$V$ spectroscopy from 1~kHz to 4~MHz, together with PUND and switching-current measurements at 300~K and 10~K, shows that the zero-bias CMW collapses as a single-Debye relaxation with $\fc = 179$~kHz in Stack~A but only $\fc = 1.4$~kHz in the WO$_x$-bearing stack. At 10~K the read window falls below 1~kHz although polarization switching persists. The combined evidence identifies an interface-limited Maxwell--Wagner process: polarization stores the bit, whereas interfacial conductivity determines whether and how fast the states remain capacitively distinguishable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
The complete figure set and compressed two-page narrative support the same central conclusion as the full TMAT manuscript. The zero-bias CMW is not governed by the intrinsic ferroelectric timescale alone: its amplitude collapses with frequency while a substantial dielectric response remains, its relaxation follows a single-Debye process, and its bandwidth freezes on cooling much more strongly than polarization switching itself. Because two stacks with nearly identical low-frequency dielectric ceilings differ by more than two orders of magnitude in relaxation time, the usable NDRO read band is set by the interface through a Maxwell--Wagner mechanism. Zero-bias CMW values should therefore be reported together with stack details and measurement frequency.
